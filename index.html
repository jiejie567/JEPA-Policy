Core innovation01

Future prediction does not require diffusion.

Future-aware robot policies are often built around iterative generative machinery. JEPA Policy takes a different route: paired latent future targets shape the same shared Transformer that produces actions, while the complete policy remains diffusion-free.

The idea

Two paired labels.
Zero diffusion steps.

Standard behavior cloning extracts only the action. JEPA Policy also predicts the future representation produced along that same trajectory—without introducing a diffusion process.

01

Paired supervision

Each demonstrated action chunk is trained with the future observation representation that actually followed it, rather than an averaged hypothetical future.

02

One shared attention stack

Action and future tokens remain mutually visible in every Transformer layer, so the future objective directly shapes the representation that generates actions.

03

Future prediction, zero diffusion

A coarse joint prediction is refined once near the demonstration manifold. Training and deployment remain diffusion-free: two forward passes replace an iterative denoising trajectory.

Action and future tokens share every self-attention layer. The future target uses the live shared encoder with stop-gradient.

Controlled evidence

The gain is architectural,
not an auxiliary-head effect.

Matching losses and training recipes while changing only information flow isolates the shared stack as the load-bearing component.

01

Shared topology

Dual-branch and gradient-routing controls remove the gain when future supervision cannot act through the action-generating stack.

02

Bounded collapse claim

An 84-checkpoint audit finds no complete representation collapse under action supervision, with the paper reporting task-level exceptions explicitly.

03

Deployment signal

Future-prediction error ranks failures within each task, supporting task-calibrated risk ranking rather than a universal failure probability.

Real-time behavior

Fast because prediction
is not a denoising chain.

Two measurements answer different questions: model-only decision time on the PPU benchmark and frame-to-action-chunk latency on the RTX 3090 robot stack. Both expose the deployment consequence of a diffusion-free policy.

Physical validation

Five tasks.
One dual-arm platform.
Every episode reported.

The 63-session study spans Cabinet, Cup Stack, Cup Upright, Plate Grape, and Pen Insert across three checkpoints per policy.

Success requires completing the task within a fixed task-specific action-chunk budget shared by every method. Wall-clock duration, inference latency, and control-loop timing do not affect the label.

Scope

Strong evidence,
bounded claims.

The simulation advantage over Diffusion Policy is positive on all eight tasks, but the mean margin is influenced by Tool Hang. The real-robot advantage over action-only is directional at ten episodes per cell.

The contribution is therefore specific: paired future supervision inside the action-generating stack improves success with little inference overhead under the evaluated configurations.
